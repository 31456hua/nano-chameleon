Each scenario contains 43 wavelengths from 390 to 810 nm, with 35 training and 8 held-out samples. The held-out indices are 2, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27, 32 and 37 in the sorted grid. The split and targets are identical across seeds and designated AGH runs. Parameters are constrained to diameter 50 to 500 nm, real index 1.05 to 2.8, and absorption index 0 to 0.35; the environment index is 1.

Normalized error is RMSE / RMS(observed training Qsca), with a denominator floor of 1e-8. This signal-relative metric makes the weak lower-bound spectrum comparable to high-scattering cases; an absolute error alone could make a near-zero signal look deceptively accurate. The denominator uses training observations only.

  • Near upper bounds: Held-out NRMSE <=1% for spectrum recovery. Parameter errors are diagnostics only; finite spectra can admit weakly identified parameters, especially near low-contrast bounds.
  • Deterministic measurement noise: Observed held-out NRMSE <=6% (about three times the prescribed 2% signal-relative noise sigma). Recovering the random noise or exact parameters is not required.
  • Dispersive model mismatch: Model adequacy diagnostic: <=5% held-out NRMSE supports this constant-index approximation on this grid; a miss is not evidence that the optimizer is defective. No constant n,k ground truth exists.

The noisy data use independent Gaussian noise with sigma equal to 2% of the latent training signal RMS, seed 20261010, followed by clipping at zero. Model mismatch uses a wavelength-dependent index and absorption peak, fitted using a constant n and k sphere. The generator parameters are retained privately; AGH task manifests exclude them.

Reproducibility and limits

This benchmark uses synthetic Mie efficiencies, not measurements of real materials. The holdout consists of interleaved wavelengths on the same grid, so it tests interpolation, not extrapolation or transfer to different particles. No confidence interval or statistical significance claim is supported by three seeds. Spectral agreement alone does not prove unique parameter recovery. Noise and dispersion were generated before search. The mismatch criterion assesses model adequacy, not exact recovery. All scores and seed outcomes are retained, including misses.
